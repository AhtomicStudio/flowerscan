# South African landrace

## Curator notes
The traditional population from around Durban that the Durban Poison seed lines were selected from. High Times
describes Durban Poison as a pure sativa landrace from near Durban.

**Why 90 (founder-approved value 2026-10-26, decision L2; awaiting `verified`).** Rubric value 90: narrow-leaf drug
type, late maturing, and no documented broad-leaf influence in the line cited. Origin latitude no longer counts
(L2), so Durban lying outside the tropics (about 30° S) doesn't lower the value.
- McPartland & Small (2020) say the South Asian narrow-leaflet domesticate (var. *indica*, the vernacular
  "Sativa", generally late maturing) spread early to Africa, and that THCV, which they link to that group, is
  common in African landraces.
- ACE Seeds sells a Khalifa Genetics line, "Durban Poison 80s", as a 100% sativa landrace from the north coast of
  Durban (KwaZulu-Natal). Its seeds trace to a grower of the 1980s, it is sold as regular seeds, it flowers in
  about 11 to 15 weeks indoors, and the page says it was never hybridised with a modern strain. That is the line
  this value is judged on (L1).

**Context that doesn't change the value (L1).**
- McPartland & Small also report that Central Asian (broad-leaf) genetics were introduced into South Africa in the
  1980s. That is a country-level report. The line cited claims no such influence, though its seeds date from the
  same decade, so a founder may want to weigh that.
- Dutch Passion lists its Durban Poison line as "Durban X Unknown Indica", sativa-dominant, ready in about 8 to 9
  weeks indoors. That line names a broad-leaf parent, so it is a seed-line dispute on the `Durban Poison` strain
  note, not evidence about this population. It no longer backs this value (T-007 had used it for 75).
